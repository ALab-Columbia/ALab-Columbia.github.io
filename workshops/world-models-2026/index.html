THE SPEAKERS

Speakers

Researchers and their talks from the October 29 mini-workshop.

OB

Oumayma Bounou

New York University

Control-aware representations and planning

Oumayma Bounou is a postdoctoral researcher at NYU working with Yann LeCun on world models. She earned her PhD through the WILLOW team at Inria and École Normale Supérieure, advised by Justin Carpentier and Jean Ponce. Her research spans world models, control, and optimization.

Research profile ↗Photo source ↗
ST

Stephen Tu

University of Southern California

Short-context prediction in dynamical systems

Stephen Tu is an Assistant Professor of Electrical and Computer Engineering at USC, with a courtesy appointment in Computer Science. His research connects statistical learning, dynamical systems, and control, with applications to robotics. Previously, he was a research scientist on the Google DeepMind robotics team.

Research profile ↗Photo source ↗
AB

Adam Block

Columbia University

Exploration in reinforcement learning

Adam Block is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Columbia University, with an affiliation in Electrical Engineering. He studies the theoretical foundations of machine learning, especially efficient algorithms for reinforcement learning, sequential decision-making, and language models. Before Columbia, he completed his PhD at MIT and a postdoc at Microsoft Research.

Research profile ↗Photo source ↗
YL

Yunzhu Li

Columbia University · Remote

Structured world models for robotics

Yunzhu Li is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Columbia University, where he leads research in robotic perception, interaction, and learning. His work combines robotics, computer vision, and machine learning to help robots understand and interact with the physical world. He received his PhD from MIT.

Research profile ↗Photo source ↗
RA

Rohun Agrawal

Columbia University

Gradient-based planning with world models

Rohun Agrawal is a Computer Science PhD student at Columbia University, co-advised by Micah Goldblum and Pavel Izmailov, and a visiting researcher at NYU CILVR. His work aims to extend the time horizons of AI agents through world-model planning and memory retrieval. He previously studied applied and computational mathematics at Caltech.

Research profile ↗Photo source ↗
YW

Ying Wang

New York University

Learning and adaptation in an open world

Ying Wang is a PhD student at NYU's Center for Data Science, advised by Mengye Ren and Yann LeCun. Her research focuses on world models, multimodal learning, reasoning, and planning. She holds an MS in Data Science from NYU and an undergraduate degree from McGill University.
